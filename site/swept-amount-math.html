Balances Equilibrium

or, my balances sing romances

One of my balances lives paycheck to paycheck. It sighs when it gets too thin and gets restless when it gets too fat. The other one sits quietly in an investment account and grows. They agreed on a few rules long ago, and since then money moves between them by itself, with no phone calls about pocket change.

The first one is the current account, a DDA (demand deposit account); the arrangement is a cash sweep. It keeps the DDA balance between two thresholds, Tmin and Tmax (just T when they are equal). Below the lower one, money comes back from the investment account (swept in); above the upper one, the excess goes there (swept out). The nominals τmin and τmax are the smallest transfers worth making: a sweep happens only if it moves at least τ. That is why the graph jumps at Tmin − τmin and Tmax + τmax. And one transfer has its limits:

  • A — available: the balance of the investment account. You cannot bring back more than is there: far below Tmin the sweep takes all of A and is still short (swept in partial); closer to it, exactly what is missing comes back (swept in full).
  • L — limit: the largest single payment the investment account accepts. Past Tmax + L one sweep is not enough, and the swept amount is not defined (overflow).

Real amounts are lopsided: thresholds in thousands or hundreds of thousands, L in millions. On linear axes that is a wok with a very long handle, so Broken cuts both axes: the pan (thresholds and nominals) stays bottom left, the end of the handle (Swept out up to L) moves top right. Each panel keeps the same dollars per pixel on both of its axes, so every slope stays at 45°: the handle is only drawn at a smaller scale. Auto picks the size by the width of the page; ?size=L, M, S or XS in the address fixes it.
