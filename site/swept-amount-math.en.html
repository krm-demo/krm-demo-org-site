Balances Equilibrium

or, my balances sing romances

One of my balances lives paycheck to paycheck. It sighs when it gets too thin and gets restless when it gets too fat. The other one sits quietly in an investment account and grows. They agreed on a few rules long ago, and since then money moves between them by itself, with no phone calls about pocket change.

In short: a cash sweep keeps the balance of the current account, a DDA (demand deposit account), between Tmin and Tmax (or at one target T) by moving money In from and Out to an investment account. Drag the handles; T switches to one target balance, $ to amounts.

  • Swept In Zero — a sweep In is needed, but the investment account is empty (A = 0): terminal failure ⏚. Like a yellow card: on the next round the plan is cancelled or paused.
  • Swept In Partial — far below Tmin: all of A comes back and the balance is still short; the investment account is emptied.
  • Swept In Full — exactly the missing Tmin − x comes back from the investment account, at least τmin (at Tmin − A that is all of A: emptied to the cent).
  • In Nominal Min — below Tmin by less than τmin: not worth a transfer.
  • In Range — between Tmin and Tmax (or right at the target T): nothing to transfer.
  • In Nominal Max — above Tmax by less than τmax: not worth a transfer.
  • Swept Out — the excess x − Tmax, at least τmax, goes from the DDA to the investment account; exactly L is still allowed.
  • Swept Out Overflow — the excess is above L, the largest single payment the investment account accepts: terminal failure ⏚.
