Balances Equilibrium

or, my balances sing romances

One of my balances lives paycheck to paycheck. It sighs when it gets too thin and gets restless when it gets too fat. The other one sits quietly in an investment account and grows. They agreed on a few rules long ago, and since then money moves between them by itself, with no phone calls about pocket change.

In short: a cash sweep keeps the balance of the current account, a DDA (demand deposit account), between Tmin and Tmax. Below, money comes back from the investment account (Swept in); above, the excess goes there (Swept out). A transfer smaller than the nominal τ is not worth making. One sweep in cannot bring back more than A, the investment balance; one sweep out cannot send more than L. When neither can help (Swept in zero, Overflow), the swept amount does not exist: a terminal failure ⏚.

Above the graph: the Threshold, written as −τmin [Tmin ; Tmax] +τmax, and the Sweep Status of the balance under the purple probe, with the amount and which way it goes.

The whole story is on GitHub, in the README of plans-math: every sweep status, every zone and its colour, why the graph jumps at the nominals, why every slope is 45° even on broken axes, and the same graph without any JavaScript, drawn in Java with a finite set of hover frames and served by an AWS Lambda (open it and move the mouse over the graph).
